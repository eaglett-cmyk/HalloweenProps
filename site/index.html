The Sleepy Candy Dragon

Tap to open

1

Deep inside a cave, at a crossroads where many worlds touch, lives an old candy dragon named Butterscotch.

He is round like a gumdrop, smooth like toffee, and he floats like a balloon. He wears a crooked purple hat with two stars on it, a gift from a traveler long ago.

Butterscotch sleeps on his candy hoard for 364 nights a year. But on one night, he wakes up.

2

On Halloween, the doors between worlds open.

Princesses from castle worlds. Robots from star worlds. Dinosaurs from jungle worlds. Ghosts, pirates, superheroes and cats. Travelers of every kind come through the doors, and every one of them finds the cave the same way: by following the warm light of the Hoard Beacon.

Over the ages, every traveler left a sweet to share. That is how the cave filled up with candy. And that is why Butterscotch wakes up on Halloween: to share it.

3

But this year, something went pfft.

A teacup-sized goblin named Snuffwick tiptoed up the candy pile. Snuffwick is shy, and he loves the dark, because dark corners are cozy for naps. So he does what he always does when he finds a light.

He snuffed the Hoard Beacon out.

In the dark, Butterscotch kept nodding off. “Zzz… a goblin snuffed my beacon… zzz…” And without the beacon, how would the travelers find the cave?

4

Long, long ago, Butterscotch hatched from a crystal egg.

When the egg cracked, its pieces scattered through all the worlds, each one a different colour. And here is the secret: every piece finds its way to one traveler.

When a piece of that egg comes near Butterscotch, it remembers him, and it glows. And glow wakes dragons.

That is what your crystal is. A piece of a dragon's egg, and it found its way to you.

5

So the travelers came anyway, crystals in hand, and set them on the Wakestone at the mouth of the cave.

Each crystal glowed. A beam of light rose into Butterscotch's chest. One eye popped open. Then the other.

“Ooh! A brand-new crystal is waking!”

Three kinds of crystal have turned up so far:

  • Dragonberry, purple and sweet, full of dreams
  • Starfrost, sparkly sky blue, full of wonder
  • Rainbow Fizz, every colour, full of giggles
6

Every time a crystal wakes him, Butterscotch rolls tonight's glow and tells the traveler where to look in the hoard:

  • Hoard Coin! The silver coin bowl. Most travelers find one of these.
  • Golden Star! The gold star bowl. Rare. “Whoa, a rare one!”
  • Dragon Gem! The dragon gem bowl, which the grown-up keeps. Legendary: the rarest treasure of all.
  • Goblin Trick! Snuffwick got him again. A rubber chicken, a fit of hiccups, or the lights going out. Candy anyway!

And every traveler, with a crystal or without, adds one spark to the Hoard Beacon.

7

A crystal wakes for exactly one child, and it never forgets. Bring it back every Halloween and Butterscotch will know you.

  1. Sparkfinder your first night: the dragon knows your glow
  2. Apprentice Butterscotch starts teaching you glow magic
  3. Glowcaster you can cast light on your own
  4. Hoardguard trusted to guard the candy hoard
  5. Worldwalker five crossings: you earn the traveler's hat
  6. Dragon Rider Butterscotch lets you ride
  7. Keeper of the Hoard the highest honour, and a secret only Keepers hear
8

Spark by spark, the beacon grew warmer all night. And when it was full, it blazed.

Snuffwick was dazzled. He sneezed. He giggled. And very shyly, he asked if he could stay.

“Silly Snuffwick… come to the feast! You're my Official Prankster!”

The travelers lit the beacon, the Halloween feast was saved, and Butterscotch waved as they went back through their doors.

“Bring your crystal back next Halloween!”

…and nobody noticed Snuffwick scampering off, hugging a lumpy sock with something golden glowing inside.

The End (until next year)

For grown-ups

Dragonglow is a homemade Halloween storytelling station: a 7-inch touch screen (the Dragon's Window) and a stone plinth (the Wakestone) with an NFC reader hidden in its top. The crystal your child is holding is 3D-printed and has a small NFC tag inside. Here is what you may want to know.

Every child gets candy, every time

There is no losing result. A Goblin Trick is a short gag at the dragon's expense, followed by candy. No jump scares, no fire, no roaring, and gentle lights (fewer than three flashes per second). A child without a crystal taps the screen for a dragon blessing and gets candy too.

What the crystal stores

Only numbers: how many Halloweens it has visited, each year's glow number and result, and its colour. It never stores names, photos or anything personal. The tag also carries a web address with the crystal's own code, which is what your phone just read: it opens the crystal's page, where its history shows up a day or two after each Halloween. That code is a scrambled number, not your child's name. On that page you can give the crystal a nickname; it is kept on our server, so please name the crystal, not your child.

Keep it, bring it back

The crystal is your child's to keep. Each Halloween it climbs one rank, from Sparkfinder to Keeper of the Hoard. Ranks count total visits, so a missed year never costs anything. The station reads the history straight off the crystal, so it works even if we replace the hardware.

The voice is AI-generated

Butterscotch is voiced with ElevenLabs and his artwork was made with ChatGPT image generation. Everything is pre-recorded on the device. Nothing is recorded from or about visitors, and the station has no camera and no internet connection on the night.

One at a time, please

One crystal on the Wakestone at a time. A first visit takes about ten seconds; returning crystals are quicker, and the station speeds up automatically when there is a line. If a crystal doesn't read, the dragon just yawns and asks to try again.

Build it yourself

The whole thing is open source under the GPL: firmware for both boards, the plinth's 3D model, the story bible, the asset pipeline and the hardware list. It is a Waveshare ESP32-S3 touch display plus a second ESP32 driving a PN532 NFC reader, talking over ESP-NOW.
